How the estimate is made

  1. Source. NASA FIRMS VIIRS 375 m active-fire detections from Suomi NPP (since January 2012), NOAA-20 (since 2018) and NOAA-21 (since 2024). The reprocessed standard product is used where available and the near-real-time product for the most recent months.
  2. UK land only. Detections are kept if they fall on UK land (Natural Earth 10 m boundary, Great Britain and Northern Ireland, buffered by m to keep coastal fires). Offshore pixels and the Isle of Man, Channel Islands and Ireland are excluded.
  3. Static sources removed. Pixels flagged by NASA as volcanic, other static land sources or offshore are dropped. In addition, 1 km grid cells detected in at least different calendar months (or distinct year-months) are treated as persistent heat sources — refineries, steelworks, power stations, flares — and all detections within their exclusion radius are removed.
  4. Confidence. Only nominal- and high-confidence detections are kept; low-confidence pixels (often sun glint or weak anomalies) are discarded.
  5. Events, not detections. Remaining detections are clustered: two detections belong to the same fire event if they are within m of each other and observed within days of each other (transitively). This merges multiple pixels of one fire, repeat overpasses by different satellites, and consecutive-day observations, while tolerating a cloudy day.
  6. Daily measures. Active fire events is the number of distinct events with at least one detection on that UTC date; new events is the number of events whose first detection was that day; detections is the count of qualifying hotspots.

What this is — and is not

This is an estimate of UK wildfire activity derived from satellite active-fire detections. It is not an official count of wildfires and should not be described as one.

  • VIIRS detects heat, not wildfires as such: agricultural and moorland management burns, bonfires, and industrial sources not caught by the filters will be included; small or short-lived fires, and fires under cloud, will be missed.
  • Each satellite passes over the UK roughly twice a day. Fires that start and end between overpasses are invisible; a fire seen on several days counts as one event but as several event-days.
  • Detection counts are not homogeneous over time: one satellite until 2018, two from 2018 and three from 2024. Event counts are more robust to this than raw detections; the Suomi NPP-only series is provided for a consistent view.
  • The most recent months use near-real-time data that is later replaced by the reprocessed standard product, so recent values can change slightly.
  • Clustering thresholds are judgement calls; the sensitivity table above shows how much the annual totals move when they change.
